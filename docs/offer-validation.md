# Offer validation plan (AB#45)

**Status:** interview-ready plan; no customer evidence recorded yet.

## Decision this work must support

Find an initial photographer segment and decide whether to launch, revise, or stop each offer independently: MIT Free Core, a paid theme or Pro package, setup/migration, and ongoing maintenance. Do not treat interest in one as evidence for another.

## Recruiting and evidence

Interview at least five target photographers or serious prospects. Seek variation in current platform, technical confidence, and whether they already own hosting and a domain. Record recruitment source and any existing relationship; friends or project collaborators alone are weak evidence. Ask permission before recording or quoting a person. Store identifiable notes outside this public repository; use participant codes here.

For each participant, record:

| Field | Evidence to capture |
| --- | --- |
| Segment | Type of photography business, volume and kinds of work, current site maturity |
| Current workflow | Site/CMS/provider, who edits it, actual monthly or annual costs |
| Switching friction | Content and media migration, SEO URLs, email/domain, downtime, skills |
| Ownership | Desired control of source, hosting, accounts, images, and exit path |
| Free Core | Would they adopt a self-hosted MIT starter? What would block it? |
| Theme/Pro | What specific design or capability would justify a separate payment? |
| Setup/migration | Would they pay someone to perform the work? Scope and budget range |
| Maintenance | Which recurring tasks and response expectations would they buy? |
| Observed behavior | Evidence stronger than stated enthusiasm: current spend, attempted migration, booked follow-up, or refusal |
| Exact quote | Short quote only with consent; separate from analyst interpretation |

Use open questions first: “Walk me through the last time you changed your website”; “What failed or took too long?”; “Who would fix a broken contact form today?”; “What would make moving your site too risky?” Only then show the offer descriptions and ask each offer's value and realistic willingness to pay. Do not lead with a proposed price.

## Analysis

Maintain a five-row evidence table in a private research record, with one row per participant and links to consented notes. Summarize patterns and counterexamples separately. Choose one narrow initial segment using repeated pain, reachability, ownership preference, and a plausible paid task. For each of the four offers record **go**, **revise**, or **stop**, the supporting participants, contrary evidence, and the next test. An interview count is a floor, not proof of demand.
